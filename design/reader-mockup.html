The photographers who shoot only in natural light

For most of the last decade, the studio light was the default. A softbox, a flash, a controlled room. A growing group of photographers is walking away from all of it.

They shoot at the edge of the day, in forests, ponds and half-lit rooms, and they accept whatever the weather gives them. The results are darker, grainier and strangely calm.

Letting the light decide

Working without strobes means working slowly. Sessions are planned around the sun rather than the schedule, and a missed hour can mean a missed week.

Several of them say the limits are the point. With less to adjust, they spend more time watching the person in front of the lens.

Editors have noticed. Magazines that once asked for crisp, even lighting are now commissioning pictures that look like they were found, not made.
